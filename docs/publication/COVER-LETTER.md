Dear Editor,

Please consider “Cost Uncertainty in Sequential Strategy Validation: A Diagnostic
Study with Adaptive Bitcoin Trend Following” as a diagnostic empirical research
article.

The paper asks how sequential comparisons lose information when they discard
shared execution-cost structure within a portfolio pair or allow a stationary
nuisance coefficient to change adversarially across periods. Two separately
specified synthetic experiments compare established evidence constructions
under known truth. The study isolates cost geometry, evidence form, volatility
adaptation and whole-history profiling, with complete uncertainty reporting
and retained unsuccessful and invalid-model diagnostics.

The audited Bitcoin application is deliberately separate from the bounded
synthetic guarantees. Its four-hour adaptive EMA selector matches the fixed
reference in the evaluation window, and shorter matched-frequency checks do
not establish material improvement. A dated, post-protocol appendix identifies
the later supplied daily SMA bot and distinguishes its delivered corrected
implementation from matched-setting exploratory controls. No trading edge,
new general theorem or universal superiority is claimed.

The manuscript is accompanied by source and results replication material,
frozen protocols, exact accounting checks and documented recorded-host
